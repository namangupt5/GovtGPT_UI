The UAE’s official legal framework prohibits discrimination on the basis of nationality, race, religion, or ethnic origin. Federal laws, including the UAE Labour Law and recent anti-discrimination legislation (Law No. 34/2023), explicitly ban discriminatory practices in employment and social life, and the UAE is a signatory to international conventions against discrimination12. The government has also enacted reforms to increase flexibility and inclusivity in its immigration and labor systems, such as the introduction of long-term visas (e.g., the 10-year Golden Visa), remote work permits, and relaxed sponsorship requirements5.

Despite these legal protections, multiple credible sources highlight persistent disparities in how immigration and labor policies are experienced by different nationalities, especially among migrant workers from South Asia and Africa:

While the UAE’s laws formally prohibit discrimination based on nationality and have introduced reforms to attract global talent, in practice, significant disparities persist—especially for low-wage migrant workers from South Asia and Africa. These groups face systemic barriers and vulnerabilities not typically experienced by skilled expatriates from Western or high-income countries. Thus, while the policy framework as written is non-discriminatory, its implementation and the structure of labor migration systems result in outcomes that are widely regarded as unfair to certain nationalities345.
